Hunter Hub — the obligation, and its receipt

The Hub answers one question: which of my gems need me right now, and who is waiting on me. So the gems are the screen. Above them sits reliability as the platform actually observes it — coverage first, as a sentence rather than a score — and reach sits beside it, visibly demoted, because tips and followers are the consequence of the job, not the job. Every gem carries a one-tap route to the composer with itself already selected.

What changed: season ranking, the Elite Hunter banner, the success-rate tile and the invented ticker are gone. Members' Ask the hunter taps now land here, aggregated per gem, which is the thing that was previously reaching the hunter nowhere.
Quiet without red: a quiet gem takes orange — an edge, a warmed ground, an orange state chip — because it is the hunter's own slippage, not an alarm raised against them. Red is spent only on open reports, which are a moderation event, and never on the same line as the Ruby badge.
Reliability is observation only: coverage, response, cadence, standing. No accuracy, no profit, no rank. Deadlines are stated times the hunter typed; no clocks, no lifecycle rails, no denominators about the project.

The gem row, in its three states

One row, three physiques. Current is a single line — logo, name, chain, followers, when it was last touched, a tick. Due gains an amber edge and opens up to show what it last said and who is waiting. Quiet gains an orange edge and a warmed ground, and is the only state that carries actions inline. The escalation is in mass and warmth, so a hunter can see which gem needs them without reading a word.

Reliability, and why reach sits below it

Four observed quantities, and a word derived from the first. Coverage is a fraction with a named denominator the platform actually owns — the hunter's own gems — which is the only denominator anywhere in this product that is honest. Everything the old Hub led with has gone.

Removed, and why

Season ranking — the full reliability leaderboard belongs on Gems, not in the hunter's own workspace.
Elite Hunter banner — a tier the level badge already states.
Success rate — it measured how often the hunter labelled a post urgent, which is not a measure of anything.
The gem ticker — a price the platform does not have.

Where the member's tap lands

On Home, a member on a quiet gem taps Ask for an update and joins an aggregated wait. That wait has had nowhere to arrive. It arrives here: one number per gem, on the row and again at the top of the gem page, and it clears the moment the hunter posts.

The loop, closed

One nudge, not 31. The member's tap is a vote; the hunter gets a single notification carrying the count, and the count is what appears here.

No member names reach the hunter. The wait is a number on both sides — private in who, public in how many.

Posting clears it. Publishing an update on the gem zeroes the wait and notifies everyone in it, which is also what feeds Response: asks that got an update within a week.

At 50 waiting the gem enters the moderation queue for reassignment. The hunter sees the same threshold the member sees, so escalation is never a surprise — and Hand over on the gem page is the graceful exit before it.

Other spaces. A moderator's Hub adds the report queue above the board and shows every hunter's standing, not just their own; the member space has no Hub tab at all. The composer opens from the FAB with no gem selected, and from any row with that gem pre-selected — plus the deadline field, which is what makes the stated time on a row possible. Project monograms stand in for real logos.
